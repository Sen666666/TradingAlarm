Stage-one notes after viewing SIX_RAW_OHLCV.png, before making state/trade
overlay. Selection was already result-based; this is consumed development.

Estun: several short up moves fail to escape the 20-26 raw-price range through
April; the strong up move occurs in May-June, with ordinary pullbacks during
that move. Initial explanation: capital committed during range failures can
be lost before a later real trend. Alternative: losses may come from genuine
earlier expansion then normal reversal, not a lack of progress; need entry
and protection dates. Never infer a fixed wait time from the plot.

Youpon: price rises in steps, particularly around year-end, then drops and
recovers, ending far above the initial quote. A negative account case within
this chart can be timing/role/exit and retry sequencing, not a bad stock.
Alternative: resource-induced late admissions may be exposed to a valid
but mature move; a tighter universal exit would also cut later winners.

Puran: repeated upward legs with consolidations, not an unbroken line. An
entirely unheld account must first be checked for missing market eligibility,
minimum-size/resource blockage, not blamed on protection. The nominal price
becomes high enough that STAR initial 200 shares could consume a meaningful
2pct trial. Alternative: no fresh CP1 during an affordable period or genuine
industry-stage/quality failure, so removing a capital gate may be irrelevant.

Pioneer: long range and failed upward legs before a late sharp rise. An actual
failed attempt can be one such failed leg, or normal temporary retreat before
the later rise. Chart alone cannot distinguish these in real time. Any exit
change needs a reentry accounting and must keep this later rise as a missed
opportunity counterexample, not call the old exit an obvious mistake.

Jiamei: prolonged quiet base, a very sharp discontinuous expansion around
year-end, then large pullbacks and a persistent decline. Tail gain may be
from pre-expansion admission/size rather than a better exit. Alternative:
an added layer late in the move can lose while an earlier layer still wins;
full-stock return and one loss layer must not be conflated.

LightPath: long flat range then rising stages from March with several local
pullbacks, accelerating late in the window. Removing normal retreat would
risk the ongoing tail. Alternative: extra profit could be larger amount or
more additions rather than more holding time. Terminal value is marked,
not realized, and the six-month tail has no evidence beyond June30.

Across the raw plots, both designated losing stocks eventually trend strongly.
The useful diagnostic unit must be actual capital attempt plus contemporaneous
structure/role; a terminal winner label cannot justify different rules on the
earlier range. No candidate policy or threshold was selected in this stage.
