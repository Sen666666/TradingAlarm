2026-11-04 loss-correction prioritization, exploratory read-only extension.

Question: Do the current C10/C20 books still lose mainly through a few >30%
closed losses, making faster exits the first research priority, or has the
problem shifted to repeated smaller losses and large unproved commitments?

Sources are the already audited complete saved layer tables for original
C10/C20 and R0 in January 2022 (19 decision dates), plus July-August 2025
(two continuous months). These are current-survivor development snapshots,
not point-in-time universes, holdout samples, or a full-cycle economic test.
The January accounts restart independently and are not stitched to 2025.

Exploratory disclosure: before writing this protocol I inspected the July-
August four-cell layer counts, largest losses, and group summaries. No new
policy or account event has been run. This protocol freezes only descriptive
aggregation; it is not a preregistered economic strategy experiment.

Use Decimal monetary arithmetic. Preserve every layer, closed/open separately.
Closed is remaining_qty==0. Final contribution includes allocated dividends;
return denominator is actual buy_cost incl fees. Report counts and gross
negative/positive closed contribution, >10/20/30% losses, deterministic worst
five and five-largest-loss shares. Report all layers grouped by actual
role/pool/purpose/entry_class, with purchase cash denominators to avoid
mistaking capital allocation for deteriorating prediction. No labels enter
any signal or action.

Classify closed layers by actual sell reasons: funding-only, protection-only
(I_structure_exit or CP1_old_line_exit), mixed, or other. This is an ex-post
description of the observed pathway, not a causal or avoidable-loss estimate.
Record full source hashes before and after; no saved account, source or old
report mutation. No account-engine imports, event replay, new indicator,
price labels, parameter search or external requests.

Stop after one aggregation and independent arithmetic verification. The
output supports priority and one proposed next finite experiment card; it
does not select a policy or claim an executable PnL counterfactual.
